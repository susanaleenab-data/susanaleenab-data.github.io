Problem & goal

Use historical purchase patterns to help explore retail performance and estimate whether a customer may not return within the next 90 days. This is an analytical risk estimate, not a certainty.

Dataset & preparation

Historical retail transaction data was cleaned and validated. The prepared transaction table contained 779,425 rows across 5,878 customers. Records without customer identifiers were excluded from customer-level modeling. The dashboard summarizes historical revenue of approximately £17.37 million and 36,969 transactions.

Features & methods

Created customer snapshots using Recency, Frequency, Monetary value, Total Items, and Tenure Days. Defined the target as no observed purchase within the subsequent 90-day period. Compared Logistic Regression with Random Forest and explored RFM-based customer segments. Worked with Python, Pandas, SQL/SQLite, scikit-learn, joblib, Git/GitHub, and Streamlit.

Validation & interpretation

Historical model evaluation used a time-based holdout to better reflect future prediction. The reported metrics below are from the September 2011 historical holdout. After this evaluation, the deployed model was refitted using all historical snapshots; the published metrics should not be interpreted as an independent evaluation of that final refitted artifact.

Application & business use

The Streamlit application provides a business dashboard with monthly revenue, country revenue, customer segments, and model comparison, plus an interactive probability-based non-return risk estimate. Results support exploratory decision-making, not guaranteed customer outcomes.

Limitations

Historical transactions may not represent current shopping behavior. The 90-day target depends on the observation window, and predictions can be affected by data shifts, limited features, and calibration. No causal impact or production business uplift has been established.
